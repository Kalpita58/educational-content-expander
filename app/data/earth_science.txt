Earth's surface is shaped by processes that act over time. Weather describes atmospheric conditions over a short period, while climate describes long-term patterns. Water evaporates from oceans and lakes, condenses into clouds, and returns as precipitation. This movement of water is called the water cycle. Rocks form through the rock cycle. Weathering breaks rocks into smaller pieces, and erosion moves sediment to new locations. Earth's layers include the crust, mantle, outer core, and inner core. Tectonic plates move slowly across the mantle and can cause earthquakes and volcanic eruptions. Scientists use evidence from rocks, maps, and instruments to understand changes to our planet.
